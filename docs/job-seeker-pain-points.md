# Job-seeker pain points and product response

Career Fit is designed around the decision sequence a person faces before applying. The same low keyword match can represent very different economic and practical situations, so the product should not collapse them into one verdict.

## Three representative users

### Early-career switcher

This user has coursework, internships, or adjacent experience but lacks the vocabulary used in the target occupation. The main pain is translation: “I may be able to do this, but I cannot show it in the employer’s language.” A generic gap list can push the user into unnecessary training.

Career Fit response:

- label adjacent evidence as transferable rather than missing;
- identify a small bridge project;
- provide an evidence prompt for converting an activity into a task, context, and result;
- separate a proof gap from a foundation gap.

### PhD or nontraditional candidate

This user may have substantial research, analytical, or communication capability but limited industry-specific evidence. The main pain is the conversion of high-level experience into reviewable proof without exaggeration.

Career Fit response:

- recognize research and project context as stronger than a bare keyword;
- show capability overlap separately from proof strength;
- flag negative statements as negative rather than positive evidence;
- suggest a portfolio or bridge artifact instead of recommending blind repetition of a skill course.

### Mid-career applicant with an unresolved gate

This user may be strong on skills but uncertain about work authorization, a license, a degree requirement, location, or an experience floor. The main pain is uncertainty about whether it is sensible to spend time applying.

Career Fit response:

- extract high-stakes gates separately;
- distinguish met, not_met, and unknown;
- place verification before soft-score optimism;
- identify the smallest confirmation action and the document or statement to retain.

## Product principles

1. **Capability is not proof.** A person may be able to perform a task while their profile supplies weak evidence.
2. **Proof is not a hiring forecast.** Strong evidence overlap can improve preparation, but it does not reveal an employer’s decision rule.
3. **Transfer is a hypothesis.** An adjacent skill should trigger a bridge action, not an equivalence claim.
4. **Missing text is not missing ability.** The interface should make this limitation visible every time it shows a gap.
5. **Hard gates deserve a separate path.** A license or work-authorization issue cannot be offset by a soft skill score.
6. **Every score should lead to a decision.** The user should know what to verify, package, build, or learn next.
7. **The user keeps control.** Structured evidence is optional, local, and inspectable; the system should never invent a resume claim.

## Why the product has three signals

The interface asks:

- **Can I do it?** Capability Signal summarizes direct and transferable overlap.
- **Can I prove it?** Proof Signal summarizes concreteness and reviewability of the supplied evidence.
- **Should I apply now?** Application Readiness combines must-have evidence, proof strength, and unresolved gates.

These signals prevent a single number from hiding the difference between ability, evidence, and eligibility.

## Safety boundaries

Career Fit must not infer protected traits, score personal worth, or make an adverse decision for an employer. It must expose uncertainty, retain negated mentions for auditability, and avoid describing any preparation action as having a known causal effect on hiring.
