# Skills investment that fits the constraints

Career Fit is a Skills Intelligence and Human Capital Decision System built by Haojian Dui. It connects reviewed skill evidence to feasible development choices. The consumer entry remains a practical job-search workspace: paste a posting and experience, review the extraction, inspect eligibility, and prepare stronger proof. The human capital workspace adds training portfolios and small-cohort workforce scenarios.

The decision question is concrete: **what can a person or organization accomplish with a limited training budget and limited time, while keeping qualification requirements visible?**

The completed public-data demonstration produces two useful decisions:

- A fictional researcher targeting an actual senior talent-intelligence posting can fund a spreadsheet model, research-translation sprint and AI verification work sample for **$320 and 68 hours**, including six hours for evidence preparation. Valuing time at the supplied $20/hour gives **$1,680 economic cost**. The role's five-year executive-advising and seven-year recruiting requirements remain explicitly unmet. A short course does not justify applying as a qualified senior recruiter; use the work samples to strengthen an adjacent-role search.
- A synthetic six-person workforce can fill **four scenario vacancies** with two direct moves and two assessed training moves, using **$1,400 in fees and 56 released hours**. Economic cost is **$3,016**. At the same $10,000 cash budget, the no-training scenario fills only three vacancies for $7,000. These differ in coverage, so the product reports the extra position and cost difference separately. With a $30,000 budget, both fill the same four roles: training reduces incremental economic cost from **$15,000 to $3,016** under the stated assumptions.

Market context comes from BLS 2025–2035 projections and 105 O*NET 31.0 skill-importance ratings across three occupations. The snapshot is bundled and works offline. National wages and projections remain separate from person-specific evidence and assumed training effects. No ROI is shown unless the user enters an incremental benefit, horizon, discount rate and basis.

The analytical contribution is a reproducible chain from public observations to a reviewed decision model, with explicit constraints, inspectable employee–role gaps, a transparent baseline and exports generated from the same result shown in the interface. It demonstrates skills measurement, data handling, constrained allocation, economic costing and professional delivery. The scenarios do not estimate causal learning effects or predict employment outcomes.
