# Human task-validation protocol

Status: instrument prepared; **no human participants or measured usability results yet**. Independent agent review and automated browser journeys are reported separately in the implementation record. They cannot establish that human users understand the product or make better career decisions.

Recruit a small purposive group across career switchers, research-trained applicants and workforce analysts. Ask for agreement to a local task walkthrough, permit withdrawal and use fictional examples. Do not collect resumes, names, health information or employer personnel data. Record pseudonymous participant IDs in a local file outside the repository.

Use five tasks: (1) identify one eligibility gate that training cannot remove; (2) change a learning-time budget and explain why the selected portfolio changed; (3) distinguish provider duration from assumed practice time; (4) record a completed artifact, transfer it for review and recover the same progress record; (5) explain why different workforce coverage prevents a like-for-like savings claim. An analyst participant should additionally change backfill or learning-effect assumptions and explain the new route.

For each task record completion without assistance, assistance needed, task duration, mistaken interpretation, severity and the participant's suggested wording. Also ask the participant to explain what the extraction F1 does and does not validate. Observing task success and the participant's explanation is more useful than a generic satisfaction score alone.

Use [feedback-template.csv](../examples/evidence_research/feedback-template.csv) as a local collection instrument. Save actual records outside the repository; publish only necessary aggregate findings after review. Report participant selection, task order, missing responses and the small sample's limits. Avoid causal claims without a suitable study design. A first small group informs iteration rather than establishing general effectiveness.
